# Harness tune — prompt template

The weekly self-improvement pass: the backpass idea implemented natively. Worker transcripts plus a metrics ledger are the loss signal, this pass is the gradient step, the human is the gate. This template IS the live prompt: `bin/render_prompts.py` substitutes the deployment config's `{{tokens}}` and `bin/install_crons.py` installs the rendered body into the scheduler — edit here (or in the config), never in the scheduler directly. It also triages the public repo's new issues and publishes the repo through a fail-closed gate.

Steps:
1. Aggregate the ledger with a script, don't eyeball it: run `python3 {{skill.dir}}/bin/trace_summary.py --ledger {{state.dir}}/worker_ledger.jsonl --since <7 days ago>` for runs in the last 7 days, PR-opened rate overall and by tier/repo, median tokens per PR-opened run, outcome-class histogram, provider failover frequency, steps-used distribution. Include the grade distribution (the ledger's additive `grade` field, a 0–100 mechanical trajectory score from `bin/grade_run.py`): low-grade PR-opened runs and high-grade failed runs are the interesting disagreements — sample their transcripts in step 2. Also list the top 5 runs by total tokens (repo, issue, tier, outcome) and each repo's share of the week's tokens: a sink that produced a merged PR is fine; a sink class that repeatedly produces failed:* outcomes is a routing/brief problem with a number attached. Fewer than 4 runs this week → light pass, say the sample is thin. Also aggregate the shepherd ledger the same way: run `python3 {{skill.dir}}/bin/shepherd_ledger.py summary --since <7 days ago>` and fold its picture into the week's stats — merges (and how many were UNSTABLE merges on pre-existing failures), reruns, fix attempts per distinct PR, and {{operator.flag_label}} flags. A shepherd action class that repeatedly produces no merge (e.g. fix attempts on the same failure class, rerun loops) is a failure class for step 2/4 exactly like a worker failure class, with the ledger rows as its evidence; shepherd rows have no run_ids, so cite repo + PR + dates for those. Also aggregate the two spike ledgers over their Go/No-go windows (brief-review spike: 2026-11-07 to 2026-11-21; catching-test spike: 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-24) — the rows whose ts falls inside the window, and once a window has closed keep reporting its final numbers until a verdict is recorded. Brief-review spike: findings acceptance = dispositions.propose_fix ÷ (propose_fix + contest + escalate + decline) summed over window rows; overhead = the ledger's tokens_out sum as a share of the window's total tokens_out (worker ledger plus both spike ledgers); the unavailable rate (outcome unavailable ÷ all window rows); the brief_changed rate; and the downstream join — for each reviewed repo + issue, the outcome classes of worker-ledger runs collected after the review, with the failed:brief-* + empty-diff share among reviewed issues set against the pre-spike flagship / re-brief cohort (report both n's). Catching-test spike: collapse the rows to the latest row per repo + PR first; rows whose label_adjudication.ground_truth is disputed are reported separately and counted in neither side of any rate; the disposition true-positive rate = (fix_brief + pr_killed) ÷ decided rows (decided = alex_disposition of fix_brief, pr_killed, or merged_anyway; pending rows are undecided — excluded from the rate but counted); overhead as for the brief-review spike (its tokens_out share of the window's total tokens_out); the Stage A kill rate = total killed_stage_a counts ÷ rows with three_state_passed of 1; survived and catch_flagged counts; and the unavailable / coverage_incomplete outcome counts. Report every spike rate with its n — these windows are small and a rate without an n is not evidence. A spike metric past its spike brief's No-go line is a failure class for steps 2 and 4 exactly like a worker failure class, with the ledger rows as its evidence; when a window has closed, your report carries an explicit Go / No-go read for that spike against the bars in its brief.
